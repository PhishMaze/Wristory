An accuracy reading will not start, or the result looks wrong

The tick is faint, so readings need a quiet room. Turn off music, a television or a fan, rest the phone on something solid, and put the watch against the bottom edge of the phone where the microphone is, caseback touching. A bed or a cushion swallows the sound. If the signal still comes back weak, try a quieter room.

Only mechanical watches can be measured. Quartz and digital watches have nothing to listen to.

The same watch gives different numbers each time

That is normal. A mechanical watch runs at different rates depending on how it is sitting, how wound it is and how warm it is. Take readings in several positions and let the app average them. These numbers are a guide for spotting changes over time, not a verdict. For anything that matters, such as deciding on a service, have the watch checked on a proper timegrapher or by a watchmaker.

How do I back up my data?

Open Stats and choose Export all data to spreadsheet. That gives you a file with your full log, collection and accuracy readings, which you can save or email to yourself. To bring it back, use Import from spreadsheet on the same screen. Photos are not included in the export.

Your data is also included in iCloud or computer backups of your phone, if you have those turned on.

Will I lose everything if I delete the app?

Yes. Everything is stored on the device, so deleting the app removes its data with it. Export first if you want to keep it.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. The only thing the app goes online for is checking the exact time, so the clock on the Today screen is accurate. Everything else works offline.

How do I remove a watch without losing its history?

Removing a watch from your collection keeps its wear history and its colour in Stats. If you want its history gone as well, swipe it left in Stats and choose Delete.

Where did the tutorial go?

The welcome tutorial and the accuracy guide are both at the bottom of the Stats tab, under About. The accuracy guide is also on the Accuracy tab, next to the Timegrapher heading.

How long does a reading take?

About 30 seconds. You can stop early and still get a result, as long as it heard enough.
